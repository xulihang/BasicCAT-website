## Translation Methods

Japanese has an enormous number of onomatopoeic and mimetic words, and many of them have no ready-made equivalent in Chinese or English. Translators therefore usually fall back on two core strategies: **replacing them with an onomatopoeic word**, or **rendering them descriptively**.

### 1. Replacing with an Onomatopoeic Word

Find an onomatopoeic word in the target language that matches the original. There are two ways to do this.

#### 1.1 Use an Existing Onomatopoeic Word in the Target Language

If the target language has an onomatopoeic word close in meaning and feel, simply substitute it.

| Japanese | Chinese | English |
|------|------|------|
| ドキドキ | 扑通扑通 | Thump thump / Ba-dump |
| バン | 砰！ | BAM! |
| ドカーン | 轰！ | BOOM! |
| シュッ | 嗖！ | Swish |
| ワンワン | 汪汪 | Woof woof |
| ニャーニャー | 喵喵 | Meow meow |
| ザーザー | 哗哗 | Pouring |
| ガシャーン | 哗啦！ | Crash! |

#### 1.2 Transliteration (Romaji) Plus a Gloss

If no suitable onomatopoeic word exists in the target language, or you want to keep the Japanese flavor, you can keep the romaji transliteration and add a gloss to convey the meaning. This approach is very common in fan translations.

| Japanese | Chinese | English |
|------|------|------|
| ドキドキ | 多基多基（心跳声） | Doki doki (heartbeat) |
| ギャー | 嘎——！（惨叫） | Gyaaah! (scream) |
| ワクワク | 哇库哇库（兴奋） | Waku waku (excitement) |

### 2. Rendering Descriptively

If there is no matching onomatopoeic word and a transliteration would only get in the way of understanding, describe the sound, state or action in ordinary words. This is the usual approach for gitaigo and gijōgo.

| Japanese | Chinese | English |
|------|------|------|
| しっとり | 轻轻地 / 柔和地 | softly |
| じっと | 一动不动地盯着 | staring intently |
| そっと | 悄悄地 / 轻轻地 | quietly / gently |
| ぐっすり | 沉沉地（睡着） | soundly |
| シーン | 一片寂静 | silence |
| ニコニコ | 笑眯眯 | smiling |
| ワクワク | 兴奋不已 | excited |


## A Real Example

Now that we have seen the examples above, let's translate an actual image, taken from volume 18 of *Detective Conan*.

![](/album/manga-sound-effect/18_094.jpg)

| Sound effect | Position | Chinese | English | Approach |
|------|------|------|------|------|
| ガラ | Top right | 哗啦 / 嘎啦 | Clack / Rattle | Onomatopoeic replacement |
| ワー | Middle panel | 哇——！ | Waah! | Onomatopoeic replacement |
| スタ (×3) | Bottom left | 嗒、嗒、嗒 | Tap, tap, tap | Onomatopoeic replacement |

In practice you'll find that these are not always familiar four-kana sound effects — the author builds them on common expressions. That is when you have to translate flexibly.
